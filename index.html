The Guy Who Sold Himself
a story about a boy who ran
flip to open  →
cover
Page One
There was this guy.

Built a little lean, somewhere between short and tall.
He liked throwing rocks into ponds,
and staring at the stars in the beyond.


He was ordinary.
At least, that’s what he thought.
1
Page Two
One day, on his way to a shop,
he found a little dog.

He picked it up, hugged it, played with it,
as if they had been cut from the same cloth.

For a moment,
he forgot about everything else.
2
Page Three
The next day, he went out into the world.

He made himself known.
He led conversations, included people,
made them laugh.

Some of them secretly thought of him as a hero.

He was clueless about that.
He didn’t think heroes could be people like him.
3
Page Four
Then he met a guy who was panicking,
desperately looking for some stationery.

So, of course, he helped.

There wasn’t really a choice.

He had to be useful.

He had an image to uphold.
He had to be good.
4
Page Five
Then one day,
a girl caught his eye.

She became the reason for his smile.

He got closer.
She came closer too.

And for once,
he let himself be human.

So...
he.......
ran.
5
Page Six
Oh, he ran.

And ran.

Somewhere along the way,
he built himself a little bubble
out of self hatred.

No one could hate him more than he hated himself.

After all,
he thought he was a dread.
A burden.
A mistake wearing the shape of a person.
6
Page Seven
Then he met another girl.

A little shorter than him,
bubbly, bright,
but he noticed the moments when her brightness faltered.

“Her brightness covers my darkness,”
he thought.

But the girl saw something else.

When she looked back at him,
all she saw were stars.
7
Page Eight
And the girl he had run from
chased him back.

He blamed himself for that too.

He learned his lessons.

But sometimes,
we carry old mistakes as though they were still happening.

We forget that learning something
means we are no longer the same person
who needed to learn it.
8
Page Nine
Once, he came to chase after another.

But when the time came,
he chose to let her go for another.

Maybe it hurt.
Maybe it was selfish in some ways.
Maybe it was selfless in others.

People can be many things at once.
9
Page Ten
He was human.

A person who held too much
and carried it for too long.

A character you’d read about
and understand.

A character you’d have empathy for.
Maybe even pride.

Not because he had always made the right choices,
but because he kept carrying what he had done
and still tried to become someone better.

Someone you’d wish would stop lamenting
and simply enjoy being alive.
10
Page Eleven
But blame doesn’t always vanish.

Sometimes we seal it away
and call it justice,or penance or deserved punishment.

We look back at the person we were
with everything we know now
and wonder how they could have chosen differently.

But the past had choices
we cannot imagine again.

Different circumstances.
Different fears.
Different versions of ourselves.

So here’s a question:

If that boy were your friend,
would you blame him the same way?

Would you call him selfish
for every time he was afraid?

Would you hate him
for every time he didn’t know what to do?

Or would you tell him
that he was only trying to find his way?
11
Page Twelve
The boy was a runner.

He ran true and true.

From people.
From love.
From guilt.

Mostly,
from himself.

But the boy was human.

And the boy is you.

So tell me...

if you could meet him now,
knowing everything he had carried,

would you really look him in the eyes
and tell him

that he was selfish too?

Or would you finally let him rest?
12
The End
Maybe he was never selfish. Maybe he was just scared.
But at the end of the day, its his choice to make.
All I can do is, hope he finds his way.
